Format Logic

Use dynamic section names, not fixed generic labels.

The model should name each section based on the actual story, not reuse “Challenge,” “Solution,” and “Results” every time.

Naming Rule

Generate section headings from the client’s old constraint, the strategic mechanism, the change created, or the after-state.

Headings should be specific enough that a reader understands the story even when skimming.

Do Not Use These By Default

Avoid thin headings such as “The Challenge,” “The Solution,” “The Results,” and “Conclusion” unless there is no better context-driven option.

Dynamic heading formula: [Old constraint] → [Strategic mechanism] → [Business change] → [After-state]. Use this formula to create story-specific section names.
Escaping the Shared-Lead TrapUse when the old system was expensive, competitive, or low-quality.
Turning Completed Jobs Into a 24/7 Local PortfolioUse when the agency created a repeatable proof/distribution engine.
From Price Shoppers to Pre-Sold HomeownersUse when lead quality or sales conversations improved.
Story Arc

The case study must explain the causal chain.

Do not write “service happened, result improved.” Explain why the work created the result.
1. BeforeWhat system, bottleneck, cost, or missed opportunity existed before?
2. PainWhy did that matter financially, operationally, or emotionally?
3. MechanismWhat did the agency change that created leverage?
4. ProofWhat measurable or observable change happened?
5. AfterHow is the client’s business different now?
Every full case study should make the reader believe: “This client was like me, this problem was real, the agency’s method made sense, and the result follows logically.”
Section 01

Hero block: outcome-first headline.

This is the top of the final case study. It must sell the outcome in one glance.

Content Rules

  • Write a specific outcome-led headline.
  • Mention the client type and primary result.
  • Do not exaggerate beyond the provided data.
  • Use “client-reported” language if metrics are unverified.

Output Fields

  • headline: one sentence
  • subheadline: 1–2 sentences
  • client_snapshot: industry, location, team size, service, timeline
  • featured_quote: strongest concise quote
Headline formula: How [Client] achieved [specific business result] by [strategic mechanism] Example: How Oasis Hardscapes grew from 3 to 11 large projects per month with an automated local social media engine
Section 02

Metric cards: show proof before the reader commits.

Metrics should be prominent, but explained later in the story.
Metric TypeWhen to UseWriting Rule
Revenue / grossWhen exact or client-reported revenue change existsLabel clearly as revenue, gross, profit, pipeline, or sales.
Lead volumeFor agencies, sales, marketing, local servicesSeparate raw leads from qualified leads or booked calls.
Time savedFor automation, ops, admin, AI, delivery systemsConnect hours saved to what the team could do instead.
Quality shiftWhen numbers are limitedUse observable proof: better conversations, fewer price shoppers, less manual work.
Never invent metrics. If a metric is estimated, label it. If unavailable, use qualitative/proxy proof instead.
Section 03

Client context: make the reader see similarity.

This replaces a boring “About the Client” section with a relevant setup.

Dynamic Name Options

  • “A Visual Local Business With No Organic Presence”
  • “A Growing Team Stuck Relying on Third-Party Leads”
  • “A Service Business Sitting on Untapped Proof”

Content Rules

  • Explain what the client sells and who they serve.
  • Include team size, geography, market, and service context when available.
  • Only include background details that matter to the result.
Write 100–160 words. Do not write a generic company bio. Frame the client in a way that makes the target reader think: “This sounds like my business.”
Section 04

Before-state: name the old constraint.

This is usually one of the most important sections for persuasion.

What to Capture

  • The old process, channel, system, or bottleneck.
  • Why it was expensive, unreliable, slow, manual, or limiting.
  • What the client had already tried.
  • What would happen if nothing changed.

Example Direction

Instead of “The client needed more leads,” write: “Oasis was paying for shared leads that forced them to compete against five other contractors, turning every opportunity into a price battle.”

Dynamic section name should identify the trap: “The Shared-Lead Trap,” “The Manual Follow-Up Bottleneck,” “The Invisible Local Brand,” “The Spreadsheet System That Couldn’t Scale.”
Section 05

Decision and strategy: explain why this approach made sense.

Do not jump from problem to tasks. Add strategic reasoning.

Decision Content

  • Why the client trusted this provider.
  • What specific outcome they cared about.
  • What made this approach different from alternatives.

Strategy Content

  • Name the mechanism of change.
  • Explain how it solved the root problem.
  • Show the logic before listing execution steps.
Dynamic name examples: “Why Visual Proof Became the Growth Lever” “Building a Local Trust Engine Instead of Buying Shared Leads” “Replacing Manual Admin With a Repeatable Intake System”
Section 06

Implementation: show the work without sounding like a checklist.

Execution details should prove competence and make the agency’s method feel real.
Subsection TypeUse ForRule
System builtAutomation, ops, CRM, content engine, workflowsExplain how the system worked and what burden it removed.
Channels usedSocial, email, ads, SEO, sales outreachExplain why these channels matched the client’s market.
Assets createdLanding pages, content, scripts, reports, dashboardsFocus on business purpose, not only deliverables.
Process changedManual to automated, reactive to proactiveShow before/after workflow difference.

Do

“The 30-day schedule turned completed projects into consistent local proof, keeping Oasis visible without adding daily work for the owner.”

Don’t

“We posted photos, videos, and tags on social media.”

Section 07

Results: combine numbers, explanation, and quotes.

Results should not feel like disconnected claims.

Result Paragraph Rules

  • State the result clearly.
  • Explain what changed in the client’s business.
  • Connect the result to the mechanism.
  • Add one quote that makes the result believable.

Example Result Explanation

“The biggest change was not just volume. It was lead quality. Homeowners were seeing the work before reaching out, which shifted conversations away from price and toward fit.”

Dynamic result section names: “From 3 Projects to 11 Per Month,” “Fewer Manual Hours, Faster Fulfillment,” “Higher-Intent Leads and Better Sales Conversations.”
Section 08

Transformation: show life after the result.

This is where qualitative proof matters. It turns the case study from stats into a story.

Transformation Angles

  • What became easier?
  • What did the client stop worrying about?
  • What could the team focus on instead?
  • How did customer behavior change?
  • What confidence or control did they gain?

Example Direction

“For Oasis, marketing stopped being another task on the owner’s plate. It became a background engine that helped sales conversations start with trust already built.”

Quote System

Clean quotes for clarity, but preserve meaning.

Quotes are trust assets. Bad transcription or unnatural wording damages credibility.
Quote TypePurposeWhere to Use
Pain quoteShows the old problem in the client’s wordsBefore-state section
Decision quoteExplains why the client trusted the providerStrategy section
Result quoteConfirms the outcome and business impactResults section or hero
Transformation quoteShows emotional or operational reliefAfter-state section
Recommendation quoteCreates sales-ready testimonial copyFinal section, LinkedIn, proof card
Allowed edits: remove filler words, fix grammar, correct obvious transcription errors, and shorten for readability. Do not change claims, invent praise, or add metrics not spoken by the client.
Length Contract

The final case study should be full enough to persuade.

A proof card can be short. The full case study should have enough context to feel credible.
Hero + Snapshot120–180 words
Before + Pain220–320 words
Strategy + Work250–380 words
Results + After300–500 words
Target full case study length: 900–1,400 words. If the input is thin, produce a shorter version but mark missing context rather than filling gaps with fake detail.
